## Eligibility and qualifying education

### FAQ 01: What is the supplement and who can receive it?

**Availability:** Suggested and available through typed questions  
**Graph event:** `FAQ_Answer_1`

The disability supplement (handicaptillæg) is an additional grant paid with ordinary SU or a final loan. It is for students whose permanent physical or mental impairment causes very significant limitations in their ability to take paid work alongside study.

You must meet the ordinary SU or final-loan conditions, attend a qualifying programme, and meet the impairment and work-capacity conditions. The authority makes an individual assessment; this chatbot cannot decide whether you qualify.

### FAQ 02: Which education programmes qualify?

**Availability:** Suggested and available through typed questions  
**Graph event:** `FAQ_Answer_2`

The scheme covers qualifying higher education programmes and Danish vocational education programmes (EUD), including qualifying EUX programmes. It does not cover general upper-secondary programmes, VUC, or vocational programmes outside Denmark that are not offered under Danish vocational-education legislation.

You must also meet the ordinary conditions for SU or a final loan.

### FAQ 03: Is having a diagnosis enough?

**Availability:** Suggested and available through typed questions  
**Graph event:** `FAQ_Answer_3`

No. A diagnosis does not itself establish entitlement, and there is no list of diagnoses that automatically qualify. The authority considers permanence, severity, current functioning, treatment and prognosis, and how the impairment affects the ability to take paid work. People with the same diagnosis may be affected differently.

### FAQ 18: Do I need to receive SU or a final loan?

**Availability:** Suggested and available through typed questions  
**Graph event:** `FAQ_Answer_18`

Yes. The disability supplement follows the right to SU and is paid together with an SU grant or final loan. You should apply for SU first. The supplement is not paid for months in which you do not receive SU or a final loan, for example because of leave, opting out of SU, or some paid-internship periods.

### FAQ 19: Can the authority approve me in advance?

**Availability:** Available through typed questions only  
**Graph event:** `FAQ_Answer_19`

No. The authority cannot give advance approval. It can only decide entitlement after receiving and assessing an application and its supporting information.


## Impairment and ability to work

### FAQ 04: What does permanent impairment mean?

**Availability:** Suggested and available through typed questions  
**Graph event:** `FAQ_Answer_4`

As a general rule, an impairment is permanent when it is not expected to become substantially better by itself or through treatment within a longer period of years. 

For some conditions that is permanent by nature, a persistence of around one year may be an assessment guideline (though not an automatic entitlement threshold).
A one-year wait does not apply to a condition that is permanent or chronic by its nature.

### FAQ 05: What about temporary illness or ongoing treatment?

**Availability:** Available through typed questions only  
**Graph event:** `FAQ_Answer_5`

Temporary injuries, isolated operations or illness periods, a single depressive episode, and pregnancy-related symptoms will normally not meet the permanence condition when recovery is expected within a manageable period.

If treatment is ongoing and the long-term effect is uncertain, a time-limited award may be possible when the evidence makes a permanent and sufficiently serious impairment probable. Ongoing treatment alone does not create entitlement.

### FAQ 06: How does some work on the side affect the assessment?

**Availability:** Suggested and available through typed questions  
**Graph event:** `FAQ_Answer_6`

The guidance says that someone who can work at least around 5–6 hours per week during study periods, including weekends, is as a starting point not entitled. Similar attention is given to full-time work for around 5–6 weeks during annual holidays.

These are assessment indicators, not automatic cut-offs. The authority considers duties, symptoms, accommodations, sickness absence and the other evidence as a whole.

### FAQ 20: Must the impairment have lasted for at least one year?

**Availability:** Available through typed questions only  
**Graph event:** `FAQ_Answer_20`

Not in every case. If a condition is permanent or chronic by its nature, the assessment does not depend on waiting for a fixed one-year period. If the condition is not permanent by definition, it may be regarded as permanent after it has lasted around one year; as a starting point, the symptoms or consequences should then have existed for about a year before the application.

“Around one year” is an assessment guideline, not an automatic entitlement threshold.

### FAQ 21: How is the severity of an impairment assessed?

**Availability:** Suggested and available through typed questions  
**Graph event:** `FAQ_Answer_21`

The authority considers the nature of the impairment, current symptoms, symptom intensity and frequency, daily functional level, the course of the condition, repeated illness periods, relapse risk, treatment effects, residual symptoms, and side effects. The assessment is based primarily on medical information and is made as a whole.

### FAQ 22: Can several impairments be considered together?

**Availability:** Available through typed questions only  
**Graph event:** `FAQ_Answer_22`

Yes. The authority can make an overall assessment of several impairments. For applications from 1 July 2025 onward, you must identify every impairment you want considered and provide the required documentation for each one.

An impairment merely mentioned in a medical record is not automatically included if you did not identify it as a basis for the application. You may add another impairment before the authority has made its decision if you state that you want it considered and provide the required information.

### FAQ 23: Does being able to work full-time during holidays affect eligibility?

**Availability:** Available through typed questions only  
**Graph event:** `FAQ_Answer_23`

Yes. The guidance says that a person who can work full-time for around 5–6 weeks during annual holidays is as a starting point not entitled to the supplement. If the programme does not have a longer holiday period, that fact can be included in the assessment.

This remains part of an overall individual assessment.

### FAQ 24: Does previous employment matter?

**Availability:** Available through typed questions only  
**Graph event:** `FAQ_Answer_24`

Yes. Employment before the application can help show the applicant's actual work capacity. The authority considers when the work occurred, whether the impairment had already arisen, why the work ended, the duties performed, working hours, sickness absence, and any accommodations.

### FAQ 25: Does work while the application is being processed matter?

**Availability:** Suggested and available through typed questions  
**Graph event:** `FAQ_Answer_25`

Yes. Work performed during the studies and while the application is pending can be considered. The authority obtains information about income and working hours from the Danish Tax Agency and considers the applicant's description of the work, duties, accommodations, and sickness absence.

### FAQ 26: Does dyslexia by itself qualify?

**Availability:** Available through typed questions only  
**Graph event:** `FAQ_Answer_26`

No. Dyslexia by itself does not establish the very significant general limitation in work capacity required for the supplement, because other kinds of student work may still be possible.

Any additional impairment must be identified and assessed on its own evidence. The answer does not exclude an applicant who has other documented impairments.

### FAQ 27: Which circumstances are not considered in the work-capacity assessment?

**Availability:** Available through typed questions only  
**Graph event:** `FAQ_Answer_27`

The assessment does not consider that the applicant spends longer on reading or preparation, has high medication or treatment costs, has dependants, has a long commute, chose a demanding programme, has many class hours, cannot find a suitable local job, or cannot find study-relevant work. It also does not assess work capacity after graduation.


## Medical documentation

### FAQ 07: What is the minimum medical documentation?

**Availability:** Suggested and available through typed questions  
**Graph event:** `FAQ_Answer_7`

For an application submitted on or after 1 July 2025, attach medical records for every impairment you want considered. The minimum is generally records from the relevant part of the health service that identify and medically document the impairment. The source may be a hospital, specialist, general practitioner or other relevant treatment provider, depending on the condition.

### FAQ 08: What should the medical records describe?

**Availability:** Suggested and available through typed questions  
**Graph event:** `FAQ_Answer_8`

Useful records describe the condition, course and duration, current symptoms and their frequency and intensity, daily functioning, treatment and effect, prognosis, residual symptoms and side effects. Work information should describe hours, duties, accommodations and sickness absence. The authority may obtain additional information when necessary.

### FAQ 09: What is not sufficient on its own?

**Availability:** Suggested and available through typed questions  
**Graph event:** `FAQ_Answer_9`

A diagnosis name, medication list, appointment notice, referral, short certificate, or your own statement will normally not be enough on its own if it does not document the impairment, course, treatment, prognosis and functional consequences.

Your description is relevant but cannot replace required medical evidence. Meeting the minimum documentation requirement also does not mean the substantive eligibility conditions are met.

### FAQ 28: How recent should the documentation be?

**Availability:** Suggested and available through typed questions  
**Graph event:** `FAQ_Answer_28`

It should be as current as reasonably possible and relevant to the impairment. The SU documentation page says that older records should still be included when they are the latest available, but records more than three years old should normally be supplemented with newer information. Material covering roughly 2–3 years may help show the course and permanence of the impairment.

The age of a record is not the only issue; relevance, the treatment history, and whether the current condition is described also matter.

### FAQ 29: Do I need to pay for a new medical statement before applying?

**Availability:** Suggested and available through typed questions  
**Graph event:** `FAQ_Answer_29`

The official material does not encourage applicants to pay for newly prepared medical statements to meet the minimum requirement. The starting point is to submit existing records that you already have or can obtain without payment, such as records available through sundhed.dk or by requesting access from a doctor or specialist.
Documentation that can only be obtained through paying a fee it NOT part of the minimum documentation.

### FAQ 30: Can a psychologist's statement be used?

**Availability:** Available through typed questions only  
**Graph event:** `FAQ_Answer_30`

A general psychologist's statement normally cannot stand alone, but it may support the application when backed by psychiatric or other medical information. If the applicant's only specialist assessment is a detailed specialist psychological diagnostic assessment, the SU website asks for the complete assessment report. The authority decides whether the collected information meets the requirements.

### FAQ 31: Should I include a discharge summary when treatment has ended?

**Availability:** Available through typed questions only  
**Graph event:** `FAQ_Answer_31`

Yes, where available. When a treatment course has ended, the documentation will normally also include a discharge summary or closing letter because it may describe the course, outcome, remaining symptoms, and prognosis.

### FAQ 32: What documentation is relevant for a congenital impairment?

**Availability:** Available through typed questions only  
**Graph event:** `FAQ_Answer_32`

Submit the most recent available medical information together with records describing the earlier diagnosis and treatment history. Relevant supplementary information can include recent support decisions, assistive devices, mentor or contact-person support, and descriptions of accommodations in education or employment. A personal statement can explain the current situation, particularly where recent medical records are limited.

### FAQ 33: What documentation is relevant when the impairment followed an accident or later illness?

**Availability:** Available through typed questions only  
**Graph event:** `FAQ_Answer_33`

Submit the latest medical information describing the assessment and treatment course, current symptoms and challenges, prognosis, and whether lasting consequences are expected. For a condition that is not inherently permanent, the duration and development over approximately a year may be important.

A recent accident or temporary illness does not automatically meet the permanence condition. A time-limited award is possible only when the evidence supports a likely permanent and sufficiently serious impairment whose final outcome remains uncertain.

### FAQ 34: Should I include a personal statement?

**Availability:** Available through typed questions only  
**Graph event:** `FAQ_Answer_34`

It is often useful. A personal statement can describe how the impairment affects daily life, work capacity, treatment, education, and the period since an earlier application. It supplements medical documentation but does not replace the minimum medical records, and is not part of the minimum requirement for documentation. 

### FAQ 35: May I redact sensitive details from my medical records?

**Availability:** Available through typed questions only  
**Graph event:** `FAQ_Answer_35`

You may cross out sensitive details such as descriptions of traumatic experiences, information about other people, or other diagnoses. Relevant information about the impairment, treatment and ability to work must remain readable.

If too much is removed and the records no longer meet the minimum medical-documentation requirements, the authority may refuse to process the application.


## Applying, decisions and help

### FAQ 10: How do I apply?

**Availability:** Suggested and available through typed questions  
**Graph event:** `FAQ_Answer_10`

Apply through minSU under “Apply for SU” and then “Disability supplement”. Apply for ordinary SU first. Identify every impairment you want considered, attach relevant medical records, and provide information about current and previous work, duties, accommodations and sickness absence. You should receive a receipt after submission.

### FAQ 11: What happens if documentation is missing?

**Availability:** Suggested and available through typed questions  
**Graph event:** `FAQ_Answer_11`

For an application submitted on or after 1 July 2025, the authority can refuse to process it if the minimum medical-record requirement is not met. It then does not decide whether the substantive eligibility conditions are satisfied.

If the minimum was met but more information is needed, the authority may request additional material. If the application is refused for failing the minimum, missing records normally belong in a new application.

### FAQ 15: What is a time-limited award?

**Availability:** Suggested and available through typed questions  
**Graph event:** `FAQ_Answer_15`

A time-limited award grants the supplement for a defined period rather than indefinitely. It may be used when the evidence makes a permanent and sufficiently serious impairment probable but, for example, the long-term effect of ongoing treatment cannot yet be finally established.

The decision states the period and relevant next steps. Any continuation is assessed using the information then available.

### FAQ 16: How can I appeal?

**Availability:** Suggested and available through typed questions  
**Graph event:** `FAQ_Answer_16`

The written decision explains how to appeal and the applicable deadline. Follow the instructions in that decision, explain which part you disagree with and why, and attach relevant supporting information that was not already available.

Use the deadline and submission channel in your own decision because this chatbot does not store case-specific details or replace official appeal guidance.

### FAQ 17: How can I follow my case or contact the authority?

**Availability:** Suggested and available through typed questions  
**Graph event:** `FAQ_Answer_17`

Use the case-status and contact options currently published on SU.dk. Keep your receipt and any reference number from minSU. If the authority asks for information, follow the instructions and deadline in that message.

Do not send sensitive medical information through an ordinary channel unless official instructions say it is secure. Contact details and processing information can change, so they are not embedded in this graph.

### FAQ 36: What is the difference between refusal to process and an eligibility denial?

**Availability:** Available through typed questions only  
**Graph event:** `FAQ_Answer_36`

A refusal to process means the minimum documentation needed to examine the application was missing, so the authority did not decide the merits. An eligibility denial means the application was examined but the authority found that one or more substantive conditions were not met.

The distinction affects what an appeal examines and whether a new application with better documentation is needed.

### FAQ 37: Can missing minimum documentation be added after submission?

**Availability:** Available through typed questions only  
**Graph event:** `FAQ_Answer_37`

Under the rules for applications from 1 July 2025 onward, an application that did not meet the minimum requirement will be refused, and later material cannot cure that application. The applicant must submit a new application containing the required records.

If the minimum requirement was met and the case is still pending, additional relevant documentation may be submitted before the decision.

### FAQ 38: What happens if I cannot meet a deadline for additional information?

**Availability:** Available through typed questions only  
**Graph event:** `FAQ_Answer_38`

Follow the instructions in the Digital Post letter and contact the authority before the deadline to request an extension if necessary. If the applicant neither supplies the requested information nor requests an extension, the authority may decide on the existing material, which will often lead to a denial because the case is insufficiently documented.

### FAQ 39: Can another person act for me?

**Availability:** Available through typed questions only  
**Graph event:** `FAQ_Answer_39`

Yes. A person can be authorised through a case power of attorney. When a representative is registered, the authority sends case material to that representative, although the applicant can still submit information.

### FAQ 40: How long will processing take?

**Availability:** Available through typed questions only  
**Graph event:** `FAQ_Answer_40`

Processing time varies. Check the SU.dk processing-status page for the applications currently being processed and the published averages. Cases requiring additional medical information generally take longer. A published average is not a promised decision date for your case.

### FAQ 41: How is a time-limited award extended?

**Availability:** Available through typed questions only  
**Graph event:** `FAQ_Answer_41`

Apply through minSU before the award expires. An extension application can be made no earlier than three months before expiry and should include newer, current medical information. The personal statement may describe developments since the previous application.

If the award has expired before a new application is made, any new entitlement normally starts from the new application month.


## Payment, work and changes after approval

### FAQ 12: When does payment begin and where can I find the current rate?

**Availability:** Suggested and available through typed questions  
**Graph event:** `FAQ_Answer_12`

If the supplement is awarded, payment is generally connected to the application date and to months in which you receive ordinary SU or a final loan, subject to the detailed rules and the decision. It is not paid for months in which there is no SU or final loan.

Rates change. Use the current rate on SU.dk rather than relying on a fixed amount in this chatbot.

### FAQ 13: How do work and income affect an existing award?

**Availability:** Suggested and available through typed questions  
**Graph event:** `FAQ_Answer_13`

Work and income can matter both when work capacity is assessed and after an award. Work before or during the case may be considered, including hours, duties, accommodations and sickness absence. The authority may obtain income and working-time information from the Danish Tax Agency.

An award does not mean every amount of work is prohibited, but work indicating greater capacity can lead to reassessment. Income also remains subject to the SU income rules.

### FAQ 14: What happens during a paid internship or after changing programme?

**Availability:** Suggested and available through typed questions  
**Graph event:** `FAQ_Answer_14`

The supplement is not paid for periods in which you do not receive SU or a final loan, which can include some paid-internship periods. Changing programme, institution or educational level may require action in minSU or a new assessment, depending on the circumstances.

Report relevant changes and check current SU.dk instructions or contact the authority because the result depends on the type of change and your SU status.

### FAQ 42: May I do voluntary work?

**Availability:** Suggested and available through typed questions  
**Graph event:** `FAQ_Answer_42`

Limited unpaid voluntary work is possible. However, if it continues for a longer period, resembles ordinary student employment, and exceeds roughly 5–6 hours per week, the authority may reopen the case to reassess work capacity.

### FAQ 43: Can I receive the supplement during a paid internship?

**Availability:** Available through typed questions only  
**Graph event:** `FAQ_Answer_43`

Normally not, because the supplement follows SU and paid-internship periods generally do not carry entitlement to SU or a final loan. On programmes where paid internship is part of the study plan, payment will normally resume when SU resumes, subject to the award period and administrative registration.

### FAQ 44: What happens if I change programme or return after leave?

**Availability:** Available through typed questions only  
**Graph event:** `FAQ_Answer_44`

The disability supplement does not automatically follow a new SU application. Apply again through minSU after changing programme, moving from a bachelor's to a master's programme, or returning after leave. Simplified reapproval rules may apply when the person recently received the supplement, but the exact result depends on the earlier award and payment history.

### FAQ 45: What happens when I move to a final loan?

**Availability:** Available through typed questions only  
**Graph event:** `FAQ_Answer_45`

After accepting the final-loan plan and conditions in minSU, contact the educational institution so that payment of the disability supplement can be activated with the final loan.

### FAQ 46: Can I receive a scholarship while receiving the supplement?

**Availability:** Available through typed questions only  
**Graph event:** `FAQ_Answer_46`

You may receive grants or scholarships alongside SU and the disability supplement. A taxable grant or scholarship counts towards your income and income allowance. If it was awarded for an educational purpose, you can apply to increase the income allowance by an amount corresponding to the grant or scholarship.
